AI Appointment Booking System

A schedule an agent can be trusted with — and the proof it can't double-book

The policies decide everything else

An AI booking agent is not a chatbot with a calendar bolted on. It is a set of rules about who may be booked, when, with how much notice, and what happens when someone doesn't turn up. Set those here and every other tab — the slot grid, the agent's instructions, its tool definitions, the messages it sends — is generated from them.

What can be booked, and by whom

A service is a duration, a price and a set of people who can deliver it. Buffers belong to the service rather than the calendar, because the fifteen minutes after a filling is a fact about fillings. Capacity above one turns a service into a class: several people, one start time.

Services

Duration is the time in the chair. Buffers are the time either side that nobody else can have.

Staff and rooms

Anything that can only be in one place at a time: a person, a chair, a treatment room, a van. Working hours are set per resource on the Availability tab.

Who can do what

A service with nobody assigned can never be booked, and the agent is told to say so rather than guess.

Intake questions

Asked once, at booking, per service. Every question here becomes a field in the appointment record and a line in the agent's script — so keep the list short enough that a person will actually answer it in a chat.

The engine, and what it will not do

This is the part an agent cannot be allowed to improvise. Every slot below is computed from working hours, buffers, existing bookings, notice and horizon — in the business's time zone, with daylight-saving transitions handled rather than averaged. Book something and see whether the rules hold

This conversation is not a language model — it is a deterministic slot-filling machine running against the same availability engine as the grid, and it says so on every screen it appears. What it tests is your configuration: whether the agent can reach a booking at all, what it has to ask for, and where the policy makes it say no.

It understands roughly what a customer types — “tomorrow afternoon”, “next Tuesday at 3”, “cancel my appointment”, a name, an email, a phone number. Where it can't parse something it offers buttons, which is what a real agent should do too.

The messages, and what they cost to send

Most of a booking system's output is not conversation — it is nine or ten messages that fire without anyone reading them again. They are edited here with the merge fields checked against the appointment record, and counted in SMS segments, because a single emoji in a reminder doubles the bill for every reminder you will ever send.
