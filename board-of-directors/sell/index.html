Why this exists

“Act as a board of directors” is the most flattering prompt on the internet.

You have probably run it. Fifteen enthusiastic paragraphs come back, they agree with each other, and they agree with you. It looks like scrutiny. It functions as applause. Three things are missing, and all three are the reason people buy this instead.

🧮

No arithmetic

A board that never divides your burn by your gross profit is not evaluating a business. It is describing one back to you in a nicer voice.

🤝

No disagreement

Real directors have conflicting mandates. Innovation wants speed, Risk wants proof. A model trained to be agreeable smooths that away — and the conflict was the value.

🎲

No honesty about inputs

Confident conclusions built on numbers you invented ten seconds earlier are worse than no conclusion at all, because now you believe them.

What it actually does

It does the arithmetic first, then lets the directors talk.

Before a single director says a word, the engine computes this from the figures you enter. It is plain code inside the file — you can open it and read every line of it. 

// computed before any director speaks
monthly gross profit  = price × gross margin
lifetime value        = monthly gross profit ÷ monthly churn
LTV : CAC             — below 1:1 is fatal, below 3:1 is a condition
payback months        = CAC ÷ monthly gross profit
breakeven customers   = monthly burn ÷ monthly gross profit
capacity ceiling      = usable hours ÷ hours to serve one customer
runway after build    = (capital − capital required) ÷ monthly burn

The last two are the ones people miss, and they are the ones that end businesses.

Capacity against breakeven

If your own hands cap you at 51 customers and breakeven needs 47, success arrives as an operations crisis about a quarter after it arrives as good news. If capacity is below breakeven, the plan cannot reach breakeven at any level of sales success — and no amount of marketing repairs that.

Three remedies exist: automate, raise the price, or add people. The app tells you which one closes the gap.

Runway against time to revenue

If the build leaves you 1.8 months of cash and your first customer is 4 months away, you run out before you find out. This is invisible in a spreadsheet that models revenue but not the silence before it.

The app treats it as a blocking finding and caps the score of the seat that raised it.

Six conditions are treated as blocking. A director who raises one cannot also report a comfortable score — the block is the headline, whatever else that mandate liked:

inverted unit economics · runway shorter than time to revenue · capital gap · capacity below breakeven · exposure above stated capital · heavy regulation with no licensing plan

The part nobody else does

Every number you type is tagged measured, estimated, or guessed.

Click a tag to cycle it. The board's confidence score is discounted by how much of its input is guesswork, and the directors say so out loud in the report. 

confidence = 0.40 × agreement between the fifteen seats
           + 0.45 × evidence quality of your inputs
           + 0.15 × completeness of your brief

That is deliberately not a probability of success, and the app says so on the resolution page in the same size type as the score itself. It measures whether the board's own inputs are worth anything. It cannot measure whether you are right, because it has never met your customers.

Guesses are weighted toward the worst case

Scenario probabilities are derived from evidence quality, not from optimism. A brief built on guesses is not neutral — guesses are systematically optimistic, and the model leans against them on purpose.

Unanimity triggers a warning

If the fifteen scores land within 8 points of each other, the app flags it. That much agreement means one input is dominating every seat, or the brief is too vague to disagree about. A board that agrees on everything has one director wearing fifteen hats.

  • You want automated bookkeeping, a business plan template, or a pitch deck. This is none of those

  • You want something that integrates with your bank, your CRM, or your accounting software. It integrates with nothing on purpose

Questions

The things people ask before buying.

Do I need an OpenAI or Claude API key?

No. The whole board runs offline on a scoring engine built into the file. AI mode is optional and additive — and even it has a route that needs no key, because the app can build the complete board prompt with your figures already in it, ready to paste into any free chat model.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, completely, and that is a design decision rather than a limitation. Open the file from your desktop with the wifi off and every feature works. Nothing loads from a CDN, there are no web fonts, and there is no analytics script.

Where does my data go?

Into your own browser's local storage and nowhere else. You can back the whole session up to a JSON file and restore it later. The product has no server, no account and no database — which you can confirm yourself, because it is one readable file.

Is this financial or legal advice?

No, and it says so inside the product as well as here. It is a scoring engine applying fixed, readable rules to figures you supply. It has no access to your market, your customers or your books, and it cannot verify a single number you enter. Anything involving other people's money, regulated activity or debt should be checked by someone qualified and accountable.

What if it tells me not to do the thing I want to do?

Then you have learned something for the price of a download rather than the price of a launch. You are still free to proceed — it has no authority over you. But it will have written down exactly which numbers have to be wrong for you to be right, which is a useful list to keep.

Can I use it for more than one decision?

Yes, unlimited. Back up each session as a JSON file, clear, and start the next one. Re-running the same decision at day 30 and day 90 with the figures you actually observed is where most of the value is — the point is watching which assumption was wrong and how fast you found out.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The layout is responsive down to small screens and there is no horizontal scrolling. Typing a full brief is more comfortable on a laptop, but reading the board's report and ticking off the roadmap works fine on a phone.

Can I resell it or use it with clients?

Client work is covered by the team licence. Reselling the file itself is not included in any tier. If you want to bundle it into something you sell, message me before you do and we will sort out terms.
