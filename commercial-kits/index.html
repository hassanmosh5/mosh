    Where this sits in the book. The AI Income Blueprint puts video generation in
  Tier 3 — "Ignore For Now" — with a one-line verdict: Runway / Sora / Kling (video generation —
  high cost, niche application), and HeyGen / Synthesia (AI video avatars — viable but
  premium-tier commitment). Its Tier 2 stops at ElevenLabs for voice-over and Descript for
  editing. That verdict is a judgement about cost, and Chapter 4 gives the rule it comes from: do not
  add paid tools before consistent income, and treat $100–$200/month of subscriptions with nothing
  earned as the mistake it is. This app does not overturn the verdict — it makes it computable for
  one specific job, then gives you the production kit for the jobs where it clears.
    
  
    What the book does support. Chapter 8 is direct about the demand: short-form video
  management "commands a significant premium — typically $200–$400 more per month than static
  content packages", and a video-first package "positions you at the premium end of the market with
  minimal additional AI production complexity". That last clause holds when the client is on camera
  and supplies the footage, which is the case Chapter 8 is describing. It stops holding the moment
  you are generating the pictures yourself — which is the case this app is for, and the reason it
  leads with cost rather than craft.
    
  
    Every number here is arithmetic on what you typed. Accept rates are planning defaults you
  can and should replace with your own hit rate after one project — they vary by model, by prompt, and
  by how fussy the client is. Safe-zone margins, duration caps and platform rules drift constantly;
  confirm them in the platform's own current spec before you export. Nothing in this app forecasts
  how the commercial will perform.
