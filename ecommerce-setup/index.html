Do you actually need your own store yet?

The AI Income Blueprint gives a platform sequence — start on Gumroad, add Etsy once you have a validated product and two to three reviews, then “add your own website once you are generating consistent income and want to reduce platform fees and build a direct customer relationship.” That last step is one line in the book. These guides are that line, expanded: when to take it, and exactly how.

A marketplace rents you an audience. Your own store rents you nothing and gives you nothing — you bring the traffic, and in exchange you keep the margin, the customer email list and the brand. Opening one before you have traffic is the single most common way people spend $39 a month to sell nothing.

Break-even: when does a monthly plan beat a per-sale fee?

A marketplace takes a percentage forever. Your own store charges a fixed monthly fee plus a smaller percentage. Below a certain monthly revenue the percentage is cheaper; above it, the fixed fee is. That crossover is the only number that matters here.

The trap. “I'll build the store first, then find customers” inverts the order that works. Every route above except Shopify puts you in front of buyers on day one. Shopify puts you in front of nobody on day one — it is a multiplier on traffic you already have, and a multiplier on zero is zero.

You can run both. Nothing forces a choice. Keeping validated listings on Etsy for discovery while your own store carries bundles, upsells and repeat buyers is the normal end state, not a compromise. The book's sequence is additive — “add your own website”, not “move to”.

The first 90 days

The book is blunt that digital product income is non-linear and that the first ninety days are slow — listings, reviews and platform SEO compound rather than pay out immediately. Your own store is slower still, because you are also building the traffic. Plan for it.

WindowThe one jobWhat success looks like
Days 1–30Get five real strangers through checkoutFive completed orders you did not personally know the buyer of. Every friction point they hit, fixed.
Days 31–60One traffic source, done properlyA single channel — marketplace cross-sell, one social platform, or email — producing repeatable visits. Not four channels producing none.
Days 61–90Second product + email captureA second listing that an existing buyer would want, and a list you can email when you publish the third.

The one metric worth watching early. Not sessions. Not conversion rate — at low traffic it is statistical noise. Watch completed checkouts per week and abandoned checkouts. Abandoned checkouts with a real email in them are a bug report about your checkout, and they are the cheapest sales you will ever recover.

Notes on specific platforms

Shopify
Using Shopify Payments where it is available avoids the extra transaction fee Shopify charges on third-party gateways. That fee is charged in addition to whatever the third-party gateway itself charges — it is the most commonly missed line in a Shopify cost estimate.
Etsy
Three separate fees stack: a listing fee per listing (which renews on a fixed cycle and again on each sale), a transaction fee on the sale including the shipping you charge, and payment processing. Offsite Ads fees may apply on top and are mandatory above a revenue threshold.
Gumroad
The book's table lists a paid tier that removes the percentage fee. Gumroad has since consolidated toward a single flat rate with no paid plan. Confirm the current terms before planning around either figure — the default here is the flat rate.
Payhip
The free tier's percentage is charged on top of payment processing, since Payhip settles through your own Stripe/PayPal account rather than paying you out itself.

Tiering beats discounting. The book's observation is that the same product offered at two or three price points outperforms a single price: the middle tier sells most, and the premium tier exists to make the middle one feel like the sensible choice. On Shopify that is one product with three variants, not three products.
