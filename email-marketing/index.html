Adding subscribers is not growing a list

A list leaks. People unsubscribe, addresses bounce, and—far bigger than either—people quietly stop opening. Gross additions is the number every course quotes. Net reachable growth is the number that decides whether this is an asset or a hobby.

How a sequence's revenue is worked out

Triggered mail is opened more than a broadcast, because the person just did something. The first email gets the engaged segment's open rate multiplied by the lift below; each email after it is multiplied by the decay. People who buy leave the pool, and so do people who unsubscribe — which is why a sequence's conversion is always less than the sum of its emails, and why adding a ninth email to a welcome sequence is worth so much less than the first.

Both are stated defaults. If your mailing tool reports per-email open rates for a sequence you already run, set them from that and the whole build order re-ranks.

        The model, and where it will be wrong
        

          

            Placement is estimated from a curve through five anchors, using the trailing engagement rate
            of what you have been sending, then penalised for complaints. Nobody outside the mailbox
            providers knows the real function — this one has the right shape and is a guess about the

Per-segment behaviour

          

            

              

                SegmentOpens a given send %Unsub / send %
                Complaints / send %Slides down / month %

            The last column is the check the defaults were set by. Each segment is defined by a length of
            silence, and that puts a ceiling on its open rate: if the figure there is low, you have
            described a segment that would have escaped its own definition long ago. It is not a rule —
            edit the rates freely — but a 20% there means the numbers are arguing with each other.
          

          

            Anyone who opens anything in a month returns to engaged. Anyone who is not sent to cannot
            open, so they slide. That single rule is what makes never mailing a segment a decision to
            lose it — which is the honest cost of pruning, and the app counts it.

      Email Marketing Automation — a planning tool for The AI Income Blueprint by Hassan
      Mohammed. The book names email twice: Chapter 15 lists building a list among its growth levers, and
      Chapter 12 treats newsletters as a media property to monetise. Neither says what to send, when, or
      what a subscriber is worth. The drafting prompts use Chapter 5's CLEAR Formula and 3-Pass Method,
      and the launch sequence follows Chapter 14's 30-day plan. Everything else — the placement model,
      the sequence library, the deliverability rules, the cost models — is email practice the book does
      not cover.
    

    

      Rates, price bands and sending costs are planning defaults with wide ranges, not measurements
      of your list or quotes from any provider. Replace them with the figures on your own dashboard and
      your own invoice; the app tells you, on every tab, which numbers are still defaults. The placement
      curve is a stated heuristic with the right shape and guessed numbers, not a model of any real
      filter. Nothing here forecasts sales: every figure is arithmetic on numbers you supplied.
