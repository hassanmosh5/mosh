From the marking room

An essay is judged on four things at once: content (are the points relevant and developed), organisation (does the piece have shape, and do the paragraphs lead into each other), expression (range and control of vocabulary and sentence structure) and mechanical accuracy (spelling, punctuation, agreement). Expression usually carries the most weight of the four — which is why an essay full of good ideas in weak sentences lands lower than students expect.

Step-by-step method

Even summary has a working — most candidates never see it

Summary is the most mechanical question on the paper, and the one most students approach by instinct. Here is the method, in the order to do it.

WASSCE EnglishSummaryEvery year

“In three sentences, one for each, state the writer’s reasons for opposing the new market levy.”

  1. Read the instruction before you read the passage. It tells you three things: how many points (three), what form (one sentence each), and what to look for (reasons for opposing). Everything in the passage that is not a reason for opposing is now irrelevant to you. 3 points · 1 sentence each · reasons AGAINST only
  2. Hunt only for the named idea. Go through the passage once and underline every place the writer gives a reason to oppose. Do not underline the market’s history, the mayor’s statement, or anything that argues for the levy — however interesting it is.
  3. Collapse repetition into single points. Writers restate. Two sentences saying “traders cannot afford it” and “the fee exceeds a day’s takings” are one point, not two. An example is not a point either — it is evidence for one.
  4. Rewrite each point in your own words, one complete sentence at a time. Lifting the passage word for word scores nothing for that point. Change the structure, not just a synonym or two. Weak: "The levy is too high for traders." (lifted) Better: "Traders cannot meet the charge because it exceeds what many of them earn in a day."
  5. Stop at three, and check each sentence stands alone. Each must be grammatical on its own, with no “and also” joining two points into one sentence to sneak in a fourth. Examiner’s note: the commonest way to lose marks in summary is writing five points hoping one lands. Markers take the first three in order — the extra two are not read, and a wrong first point is simply a wrong first point.

Where the marks were: point identification, and expression in your own words. Two marks per point, and the second one is the one candidates forfeit by copying.
