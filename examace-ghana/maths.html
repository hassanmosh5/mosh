From the marking room

The three places we most often watch candidates throw away marks in Maths: rounding too early and carrying the error through, giving an answer without the unit, and answering a different question from the one asked (finding d when the paper asked for S₂₀). None of those three is a knowledge problem. All three are trainable in a fortnight — and the plan trains them from day one.

Step-by-step solutions

Every question opens up like this

Not just the answer. The order of the working, the point where the marks sit, and the mistake most candidates make at that exact line.

WASSCE Core MathsBearings & trigonometryHigh frequency

A boat sails 15 km from port P on a bearing of 060° to a point Q, then 20 km from Q on a bearing of 150° to a point R. Calculate |PR|, correct to three significant figures.

  1. Draw it, and draw the north line at every vertex. A sketch with north lines at P and at Q carries a mark on its own, and without the one at Q you cannot find the angle. Bearings are always measured clockwise from north.
  2. Find the back-bearing from Q to P. If P to Q is 060°, then Q to P is 060° + 180°. Bearing of P from Q = 240°
  3. Get angle PQR by subtracting the two bearings at Q. Both are measured from the same north line at Q, so the angle between them is just the difference. ∠PQR = 240° − 150° = 90°
  4. Notice what that means before you reach for a formula. The triangle is right-angled at Q. You do not need the cosine rule here — Pythagoras is faster and harder to slip on. |PR|² = |PQ|² + |QR|²
  5. Substitute, then answer in the form asked for. |PR|² = 15² + 20² = 225 + 400 = 625 |PR| = √625 = 25 |PR| = 25.0 km (3 s.f.) Examiner’s note: the question said three significant figures, so “25” is not the finished answer — write 25.0 km. And the unit is part of the mark, not decoration.

Where the marks were: the sketch, the back-bearing, the angle, the method, the stated answer. A candidate who gets 625 but writes no working has thrown away most of them.
