Invoice & Payment Automation

Send it right, chase it on a schedule, watch the money land

The policy every invoice inherits

Almost everything that makes invoicing feel like admin is a decision you are re-making each time: what to number it, when it is due, when to chase, what to charge for being late. Decide once here and every invoice, reminder and due date in the other eight tabs follows from it.

1 · Who is billing

Printed at the top of every invoice. Whatever your tax authority expects to see, put it here.
Left blank it is omitted from the invoice rather than printed empty.
Fills {{your_name}}. Every chase message uses it, and the queue refuses to call a message ready to send while it is empty.

2 · Numbering

A sequence with no gaps and no repeats. Most tax regimes require both.
Do not renumber a sent invoice. If one is wrong, issue a credit note against it and raise a new one. Editing history is what turns a bookkeeping question into an audit.

3 · Default payment terms

The due date is derived from this, never typed. A client can override it; an invoice can override the client.

4 · Tax

Applied per line, so a zero-rated line and a standard-rated line can sit on the same invoice.
Both are accepted in most regimes and they can differ by a cent. Pick the one your accounting software uses and stop thinking about it.

5 · Chase sequence

When a reminder goes out, on what channel, in what tone. Offsets are days relative to the due date.

6 · Late fees

Only enforceable if it is on the invoice before the work starts. The app prints it on every invoice when switched on.
Caps and statutory rates vary by country and by whether your client is a business or a consumer. The UK and EU set a statutory rate (a central-bank base rate plus a margin) plus fixed compensation per invoice; several US states cap the rate outright. Enter what your contract and your jurisdiction allow — this app does the arithmetic, it does not know the law where you are.

7 · Currency and money

Every amount in this app is held as a whole number of the smallest unit — cents, pence, kobo — and never as a decimal.
Set per invoice too. Totals are never summed across currencies.
Merge fields work here too — {{invoice_number}} is the useful one, since a payment without a reference is a payment you can't match.

Who you bill, and on what terms

Terms belong to the client, not to your memory of the client. Anything left as default follows the Setup tab, so changing the house policy moves everyone who hasn't been given their own.

Clients

Invoices

Status is derived, never typed: an invoice is paid when its payments cover it, overdue when today is past its due date and they don't. There is no way to have a "paid" invoice with money outstanding, because nobody gets to set that field.

What to send today

Every unpaid invoice runs the same schedule: each step lands on a date derived from the due date, and the app works out which one is owed as of today. The message comes out written, with the client's name, the amount, the dates and the reference already in it.

Invoices that raise themselves

Retainers, subscriptions and monthly maintenance don't need deciding each month — they need issuing on a date. Set the schedule once and the app tells you which ones are due, then generates the invoice with a click.

What actually lands in the account

An invoice is a claim; a payout is money. Between the two sit a percentage, a fixed fee, a cross-border surcharge and a wait. This tab prices that gap for each method, and works the arithmetic backwards when you need a specific amount to land.

Aging and expected cash

Owed is not the same as coming. The aging buckets say how old the debt is, the schedule says when it is contractually due, and the two together are the only honest answer to "can I pay myself this month".

Making it run without you

This page is a place to decide the policy and see it working. Running it every week is a job for a tool that can send email at 6am. Each automation below is written as a trigger, an action and the thing the tool can't do — with the setup path for the usual suspects.

The whole policy, as one document

Everything you chose, written out: terms, numbering, tax, the chase sequence with its message templates, the late-fee policy, and the current ledger. Hand it to a bookkeeper, paste it into a contract, or keep it as the answer to "what are our payment terms".

Invoice & Payment Automation — a single HTML file. No network requests, no accounts, no build step. Everything you enter stays in this browser's local storage until you export or clear it.

The arithmetic here is arithmetic: totals, dates, interest and gateway fees. It is not legal or tax advice. Invoice content requirements, statutory late-payment rates, interest caps and surcharging rules differ by country and by the kind of customer you are billing — check yours, and put what you find into the Setup tab.

Published card-processing rates change without notice; the figures in Getting paid are starting points to replace with your own statement, not quotes.
