    Where the sale actually comes from
    
    
    The book's Chapter 9 sells local business services on the back of a free audit rather than a pitch. The same trade applies to sites — this studio is fast enough that the demo is the audit.
    
    
      
      
    1. Pick ten businesses with a bad or missing site. Search your own town for the trade and skip anyone already ranking well. Bad sites are easy to spot on a phone: pinch-zoom text, no visible phone number, "Copyright 2016".
      2. 
      
    2. Build the demo before you make contact. Ten minutes each in this studio, using their real name, phone number, services and area — everything is public on their listing.
      3. 
      
    3. Lead with the phone number question. "Built you something — is this the right number?" gets replies that "I offer web design services" never will.
      4. 
      
    4. Charge for the go-live, not the demo. The demo is already built and costs you nothing to give away. What they're buying is the domain, the hosting, the real content, the Google Business Profile link-up and someone to call when it needs changing.
      5. 
      
    5. Ask for the referral at the go-live call, while it is still impressive. Trades know every other trade in town, and that is the entire growth engine of this business.

    What every exported site does
    
      
      
        
    • One file, no requests. CSS and JS are inline, the artwork is CSS and inline SVG, and nothing is fetched from anywhere — so there is no font flash, no CDN outage and no cookie banner to argue about.
      • 
        
    • Phone-first. Single column under 700px, tap targets over 44px, a sticky call bar on mobile, and the number in the header at every width.
      • 
        
    • Findable. Title and meta description, Open Graph and Twitter cards, canonical link, and LocalBusiness JSON-LD carrying address, phone, hours, service area and price bracket.
      • 
        
    • Reachable. Skip link, landmarks, real labels on every field, visible focus rings, and colour combinations checked against the accent you chose.
      • 
        
    • Printable. A print stylesheet that drops the navigation — because someone will print the services page and pin it up.
      • 
        
    • Polite. Animation disabled under prefers-reduced-motion, and optional dark mode that follows the visitor's system setting.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Ranking in one town
    
      
    A local site does not compete with the whole internet. It competes with four other businesses in the same postcode, which is a fight you can win in a week.
    
      
      
        
    1. Google Business Profile first. It outranks the website for the business's own name and drives the map pack. Claim it, fill every field, match the name, address and phone exactly to the site.
      2. 
        
    2. One page per money service once the single page is live. "Boiler repair in Northgate" beats a services list for anyone actually searching that.
      3. 
        
    3. Put the town in the title tag — the studio does this by default. Trade plus town is the whole local SEO game.
      4. 
        
    4. Reviews are the ranking factor you can influence today. The exported site links straight to the client's review page; asking at handover is what fills it.
      5. 
        
    5. Consistent citations. Same name, address and phone on the site, the profile, the directories and the van.

    What this deliberately does not do
    
    
    Being honest about the edges is what makes the rest trustworthy.
    
    
      
      
    • No CMS. Edits mean editing HTML — which is exactly why the care plan in the Sell It tab exists. If a client genuinely needs to post weekly, hand them a platform instead of this.
      • 
      
    • No booking engine, no payments, no menus that sync. The enquiry form emails or posts, and that is all. Restaurants that need live table inventory need a booking provider — link it from the button.
      • 
      
    • No photos. The artwork is generated from CSS, so the file stays small and nothing is licensed from anywhere. Real photos of real work will always outperform it; add them at handover.
      • 
      
    • No multi-page site. One page, deep sections. That is the right shape for a first local site and the wrong shape for the tenth.
      • 
      
    • No invented facts. The demo content is fiction, clearly fictional numbers and example.com addresses included, so nothing ships by accident. The testimonials in particular must be replaced with real ones.
