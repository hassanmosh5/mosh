An order is not free to accept

Every order arrives with a bill of minutes attached: reading it, checking the payment, picking, packing, buying postage, answering “where is it?”, reconciling the payout. Nobody invoices you for those minutes, so most stores never count them — and then wonder why a 45% gross margin does not feel like one. Count them here first. Every other tab is arithmetic on this.

Automate in payback order, not excitement order

Everything here is a real order-desk automation with a build cost attached. Turn one on and the pipeline loses the minutes it removes; the payback is the build hours divided by the hours it saves each week. Two of them save no time at all and say so.

The library

What the payback figure means

Payback weeks = build hours ÷ hours saved per week. The hours saved are measured on top of whatever is already turned on, because two automations that cut the same step do not each save it. Turn one on and the others re-rank themselves.

The queue, in weeks

The automations you have turned on, sequenced by payback and by what has to exist first, against the hours you actually have. The cumulative line is the honest one: for the first few weeks you are spending time, not saving it.

Build cost is build hours × your build rate. It is a real cost even when you do the work yourself — that is the whole point of counting it.

The abnormal path is where the money goes

A clean order costs minutes. A broken one costs minutes and money — a reship, a refund, a chargeback, a goodwill credit. Set each one's policy here, and let the threshold decide the small claims instead of you.

The policy, written out

Every message you don't send becomes a ticket

“Where is my order?” is not a support problem, it is a communication gap with a stamp on it. Each proactive message closes part of the gap; the deflection compounds, with diminishing returns, and lands back on the pipeline as fewer WISMO minutes.

The sequence

Deflection is the share of WISMO contacts this message removes. The defaults are planning estimates with wide ranges; the only way to know yours is to turn one on and count tickets for a month.

E-Commerce Order Automation — an operations tool for The AI Income Blueprint by Hassan Mohammed. Chapter 7 gets you selling and Chapter 9 teaches you to automate somebody else's business; this app points that same audit at your own order desk, which is the part neither chapter covers. The build-plan sequencing follows Chapter 15's argument that the first £1,000 month is a problem of action and the first £5,000 month is a problem of systems.

Every frequency, minute figure, deflection rate and cost in this app is an editable planning default with a wide range, not a benchmark for your store and not a quote from any platform or carrier. Replace them with a month of your own numbers — the tab you are on will recalculate. Nothing here forecasts sales, and no automation on the Automations tab raises revenue: they remove minutes and losses from orders you already have.

Everything you type stays in this browser's local storage. No accounts, no network requests, no analytics.
