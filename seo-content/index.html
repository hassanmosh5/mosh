How the position estimate works

This is the app's own model, not an industry standard. It is worth understanding before you trust a forecast built on it.

The gap between your authority and a keyword's difficulty is mapped onto a position by a logistic curve. At parity you land around position 9 — bottom of page one. Ten points of authority above the difficulty moves you to about position 4; twenty points below drops you to about 38.

The curve is monotonic and has no cliff edges, which matters: a model with bands makes a keyword at KD 39 look categorically different from one at KD 41, and that difference isn't real.

Topical clusters

Group the keywords into clusters on the Keywords tab and this builds the map: one pillar page per cluster, spokes beneath it, and the internal links that connect them. It also runs the two checks nobody runs until traffic drops — whether two of your keywords are really the same page, and whether any page has nothing linking to it.

Cannibalisation check

—

Two keywords with the same intent and heavily overlapping wording will be answered by the same page, whatever you intended. Give them separate URLs and they compete with each other: the two pages split the links and the relevance signals, and neither ranks as well as one page would have.

The map

The pillar is the broadest keyword in the cluster — highest volume, least specific intent. Everything else hangs off it.

Internal link plan

Every link the map implies, in both directions, with the anchor text to use. Descriptive anchors, not "click here" and not the exact keyword forty times — the anchor is a relevance signal and an identical one on every link reads as what it is.

    Outline

    Prompts

    Each one carries your angle, audience and voice. A prompt without those three returns the same article everybody else in your niche is publishing.

    On-page

    The title and description are measured the way they are actually truncated — by pixel width, not by character count. A 60-character title full of capitals and Ws is cut off; a 65-character one full of thin letters is not.
