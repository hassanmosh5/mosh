The business, and the numbers the plan hangs on

The business type decides the pages, the pipeline stages, the CRM fields and which automations are even relevant. The numbers decide which of those automations are worth building — at low volume most of them are not, and this page is where that gets settled rather than assumed.

3 · Volume and value

Everything on the Automations tab is arithmetic on these four figures. Estimate them from the last three months, not from a good month.

The site, page by page

A website for this kind of business is a lead-capture machine with some reading material attached. Each page below has exactly one job and exactly one action; the sections are in the order they should appear, and each page carries a copy brief you can hand to an AI or a writer.

The pipeline

A stage exists to answer one question: what has to be true for this to move on, and who does it. Stages without entry rules turn a CRM into a list of people you feel vaguely bad about.

What the CRM actually stores

Every field here is used by something — a message, an automation condition, or a report. Fields that aren't used by anything are how a CRM becomes an unloved form nobody fills in, so the table says which automations and messages depend on each one.

Automations, with the payback maths shown

Each one is a real recipe — trigger, conditions, actions, and the way it fails. The figures come from your volume and your hourly rate: how often it runs, the time it gives back, what it costs to build, and how long that takes to earn back. Tick the ones you're building.

The stack, and what it costs to keep running

Twelve layers, a default per tier, and every price editable — because published pricing moves and this file doesn't. The total is the number that has to be smaller than the time you're buying back.

The messages the automations send

Written to be sent by a small business, not by a marketing department. Every {{merge_field}} is checked against the CRM fields on the Fields tab — a template that references a field you don't collect sends an email addressed to nobody.

What to build, in what order

Ordered by dependency first and payback second, in four phases. Phase one is the part that pays for the whole thing; everything after it is optimisation, and can wait until phase one has survived a month of real leads.

Before you point real enquiries at it

An automation that fails silently is worse than no automation, because you stop checking. These are the tests that catch the failures that actually happen — duplicates, bounces, loops, opt-outs, and the day the person who built it leaves.

The whole build as one document

Everything above as Markdown, JSON or CSV: the site plan, the pipeline, the field schema, the selected automations with their recipes, the stack with costs, and the message templates. Hand it to a builder, paste it into an AI, or read it back in six months when you've forgotten why stage four exists.

Everything stays on this device. What you type is saved to this browser's local storage. Nothing is uploaded, and this file makes no network requests of any kind.

The maths is arithmetic on your own figures. Time saved, payback and break-even volume are computed from the numbers you entered on the Setup tab and the per-run estimates in each recipe. They are estimates of effort, not a forecast of revenue. Tool prices are list prices noted at the date shown on the Stack tab and are editable because they will be wrong eventually.
