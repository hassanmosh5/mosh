The conversation, written down before the model sees it

A sales assistant that has not been told what a good conversation looks like will produce a polite one instead. Here you write the stages, the questions that decide whether someone is worth quoting, and the answer to every objection you already know is coming. This is the raw material for the system prompt, and it is what the dry run tests.

The 24 hours that decide what a follow-up costs

    

      WhatsApp is not email. When a customer messages you, a customer service window opens for
      24 hours, and inside it you can write whatever you like, as often as you like, for nothing. When
      it shuts, free-form messaging stops: to reach that person again you need a message template,
      approved in advance, in a category, at a price — and if the category is marketing, you need
      their consent as well. Every follow-up sequence anyone has ever sketched on a whiteboard runs into
      this rule at step three.

Templates, checked before you submit them

      runs those rules against what you have typed, offline, in the second it takes to type it —
      including the one nobody expects, which is that a template's category is decided by what
      the message says, not by what you selected.

    What it has to earn before it has earned anything
    
    
    
      Nobody can tell you how much an assistant will lift your conversion rate, and this app will not
      pretend to. It does the arithmetic the other way round: given your funnel, your prices and what
      the messages cost, it computes the lift the assistant has to produce to break even. That
      number you can judge. Anything else on this page is an assumption you typed.

    What this app is, and what it is not. It is a design tool. It writes the brief, checks the
    templates, does the window arithmetic and the cost model. It does not connect to WhatsApp, does not
    send anything, and contains no model — the dry run is a rehearsal against your own written
    answers, not a conversation.
  
    
  
    
    On the platform rules. The window length, template categories, opt-in requirements and
    per-message pricing described here are Meta's, not this app's, and Meta changes them. The figures
    are a 2025 snapshot and every one is editable. Before you build, check the current rules and the
    current rate card; before you launch, check them again.
  
    
  
    
    Where it comes from. The qualification-before-quoting structure, the price-the-outcome
    framing and the follow-up discipline are from The AI Income Blueprint by Hassan Mohammed
    (Chapters 5, 7, 11 and 14). The platform mechanics, the template rules and the cost model are not in
    the book — they are the part you have to get right anyway.
  
    
  
    
    Nothing you type here is transmitted anywhere. It is saved in this browser's local storage and
    nowhere else. Clearing your browser data deletes it; Back up is the only copy.
